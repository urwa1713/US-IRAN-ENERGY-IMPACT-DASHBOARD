## 📌 About the Project

The **US–Iran Energy Impact Dashboard** is a data analytics and visualization project designed to explore how geopolitical developments involving the United States and Iran can affect energy markets and petrol prices.

The project transforms historical and event-based energy data into an interactive dashboard, making it easier to identify **price movements, trends, fluctuations, and potential relationships between geopolitical events and energy-market behavior**.

The main objective is to move from raw data to an interactive analytical story rather than simply presenting a collection of charts.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The dashboard aims to answer questions such as:

- 📈 How have energy and petrol prices changed over time?
- 🌍 What major geopolitical events coincide with significant market movements?
- 🛢️ How does crude-oil price movement relate to petrol prices?
- ⚠️ Which periods show the largest price fluctuations?
- 📊 What patterns can be observed before and after major US–Iran developments?
- 🔎 Can geopolitical events help provide context for unusual energy-price movements?

# 💡 Why This Project Matters

Energy markets are highly sensitive to geopolitical developments.

Events involving major oil-producing regions can affect:

- Crude oil supply expectations
- Market sentiment
- Transportation costs
- Refining economics
- Energy-importing economies
- Consumer fuel prices

Rather than looking at these factors independently, this project attempts to bring them together into a **single visual analytical story**.

---

# 📊 Dashboard Design Philosophy

The dashboard is designed around three principles:

### 1. 📌 Context

Price changes are presented alongside relevant geopolitical developments.

### 2. 🔎 Comparability

Interactive visualizations make it easier to compare different periods and indicators.

### 3. 💡 Accessibility

Complex energy-market information is transformed into visual insights that can be explored without working directly with the raw dataset.

---

# ⚠️ Important Analytical Note

A correlation between a geopolitical event and a change in energy prices does **not necessarily establish causation**.

Energy prices can simultaneously be influenced by many factors, including:

- Global supply and demand
- OPEC+ decisions
- Production disruptions
- Inventory levels
- Shipping conditions
- Currency movements
- Economic expectations
- Market speculation
- Other geopolitical events

Therefore, this dashboard should be interpreted as an **exploratory analytical tool**, not as a causal econometric model.
